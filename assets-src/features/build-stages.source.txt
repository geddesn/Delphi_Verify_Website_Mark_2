⚠️  THESE SIX ARE A SEQUENCE, NOT SIX PICTURES
Every other asset on the trust stage simply stands there. These dissolve from
one into the next, so they have to stay REGISTERED: the island outline, the
jetty and the villa footprint must not drift between stages, or the dissolve
reads as a cut to a different island.

HOW THEY WERE SPLIT — READ BEFORE RE-CUTTING
The six are packed tight enough in the source that they interlock: each island
is a rounded diamond, and adjacent rows touch. A flood fill over the whole
image finds TWO components, not six — one per column — because the three
islands in a column are joined.

So the split is not a plain grid cut:

  columns   a fully transparent gutter runs the whole height at x = 764-777,
            so the two columns separate cleanly.

  rows      cut at the WAIST — the narrowest point between two islands —
            rather than at the exact third, because that is where a cut takes
            the least of either. Measured per column, since the two disagree
            by a few pixels: column 1 at y 354 and 690, column 2 at y 358
            and 692.

  cleanup   within each cell, keep only the LARGEST connected mass. The waist
            cut still leaves a sliver of the neighbouring island in most
            cells, and this drops it.

  window    every stage is then written as the same 768x341 window taken at
            the same offset inside its own cell. THIS is what holds the six in
            register — not each island's own bounding box, which is unusable
            as a common reference because several islands are clipped, by
            their neighbours and by the canvas.

Measured afterwards, the island lands at x 10-17 → 749-763 and y 0-17 →
336-340 across the six: within about 7px horizontally and 17px vertically of
1536, which is a few pixels at the size the stage draws them.

KNOWN CLIPPING

ASPECT, AND WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE STAGE
2.252:1 — much wider than the yacht's 1.640 or the townhouse's 1.320. The
trust stage sizes an asset by assetBox, and matching the yacht's VERTICAL
extent (y 37.1-84.9) would make these 60.5% of the stage wide, which crowds
the counterparty panels on both sides. Matching the yacht's WIDTH instead
(44%) gives a vertical extent of only y 43.6-78.4. Neither is obviously right;
it needs deciding against the finished script and the anchors derived after.
