THE SET IT REPLACED, and why
An earlier 2x3 grid supplied the same morning. Its six islands were packed
tight enough to interlock — a flood fill over the whole image found TWO
components, one per column, because the islands in a column touched — and
several were clipped by their neighbours and by the canvas. Stages 1 to 4 lost
up to 514px of their bottom row.

The old masters are kept beside this file in build-v1/ (gitignored, so local
only). The old WebPs are recoverable from git at commit db522e2.

Before that again, a Mediterranean version — terracotta, cypress, olive trees.
The brief called for the Maldives.

THE SIX, in reading order, which is also build order:

  2 foundations  slabs, footings and the pool structure set out

  6 handover     mature palms, jetty, furnished and complete

⚠️  THESE SIX ARE A SEQUENCE, NOT SIX PICTURES
The stage dissolves one into the next IN PLACE, so the island outline and the
villa footprint must not drift between them, or the dissolve reads as a cut to
a different property.

HOW THEY WERE SPLIT
The six are cleanly separated in this render — a flood fill finds exactly six
— so the columns and rows cut at the exact thirds and half. Each cell then
keeps only its largest connected mass, which is belt and braces: it drops the
one-pixel bleed where stage 5's shadow crosses a cell boundary.

REGISTERED ON THE WATERLINE, NOT ON THE CELL — read before re-cutting
The islands GROW. Vegetation is taller at handover than at foundations, so
their boxes differ by 143px top to bottom (276 tall at plot, 419 at handover).
Aligning them by cell would drift the whole island downward as the trees came
in. Instead every island is placed so its BASE lands on a common line, and the
planting grows upward out of a beach that stays still — which is what actually
happened on the site.

  canvas    512 x 435, aspect 1.177
  base      every island's lowest pixel at y = 427
  margin    8px above the tallest

Verified after extraction: all six bases at 427, and edge-pixel counts of zero
for five of the six. Stage 5 has 27 pixels touching the left edge — its island
begins 1px before its cell boundary in the source. It is not visible.

ASPECT, AND WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE STAGE
1.177:1 — far squarer than the set it replaced (2.252:1), and squarer than the
yacht (1.640) or the townhouse (1.320). So the scene's assetBox width LOOKS
smaller than the yacht's and is not: at 33% of the stage this asset carries
more pixels than the 46% the flat version needed.

Vertical is what binds. The construction stage carries a payment bar across
the top and a record panel below it, and the narration owns the bottom 19%,
leaving roughly y 31-81 for the asset. See assetBox in content/trust-build.ts,
which records how the number was derived and warns that the anchors are
percentages of that box.
