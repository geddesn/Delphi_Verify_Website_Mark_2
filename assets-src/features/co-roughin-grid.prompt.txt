Photorealistic construction record photography presented as a 3x2 GRID OF SIX
SEPARATE PHOTOGRAPHS in one image, nobody present in any frame.

THE GRID
Three columns, two rows. Each cell is a complete photograph filling its cell
to all four edges. Cells butt edge to edge. No border, gutter, white strip,
dividing line, frame, rounded corners, drop shadow or numbering.

ONE BUILDING
A modest, ordinary new-build apartment in Colombia, inland tropical city, mid
rise residential tower. Grey concrete block walls, poured concrete slab floors
and soffits, rough plaster patches, steel window openings with no glass yet.
Plain and economical, NOT a luxury villa: no stone, no marble, no timber
panelling, no architectural flourish. Working daylight through unglazed
openings. Dust, offcuts and chalk marks as a real site has them.

THE SIX

1 TOP-LEFT. KITCHEN PLUMBING. A kitchen wall in bare blockwork with white PVC
waste pipes and white water supply points chased in and clipped, capped off,
plus a yellow gas line, before any render. Square-on to the wall, the floor and
both corners visible.

2 TOP-CENTRE. BATHROOM PLUMBING. A small bathroom in bare block with sanitary
points roughed in - waste stub for the pan, supply points for basin and shower
- and a dark grey waterproofing membrane applied across the floor and turned
up the walls. Square-on from the doorway.

3 TOP-RIGHT. LIVING ROOM ELECTRICAL. A living room wall and ceiling soffit with
orange corrugated electrical conduit routed and clipped across the concrete,
octagonal outlet boxes set at skirting and switch height, wires pulled through
and coiled. Square-on.

4 BOTTOM-LEFT. BEDROOM ELECTRICAL. A bedroom corner with conduit runs dropping
down the block wall to two outlet boxes, and a ceiling rose box in the soffit.
Clearly a smaller, plainer room than cell 3. Square-on.

5 BOTTOM-CENTRE. KITCHEN ELECTRICAL. The kitchen again from a different wall:
heavier appliance circuits in conduit, three outlet boxes in a row at worktop
height, a thicker feed running up to the ceiling. Square-on.

6 BOTTOM-RIGHT. DISTRIBUTION BOARD. A small entrance hall with a plastic
consumer unit open on the wall, breakers visible in a neat row, conduit
entering from above, the cover hanging or set aside. Square-on, close enough to
read the board as a board.

HOW THEY ARE PHOTOGRAPHED
As site record photographs, not as architecture. Square-on, verticals true,
even light, everything legible including the corners and the floor. Taken on a
phone by a contractor documenting their own work. No shafts of sun, no
vignette, no shallow depth of field, no artful crops.

STRICTLY NOT
No people anywhere, including reflections. No labels, captions, arrows,
numbering or watermarks. No text of any kind. No finished surfaces: nothing in
this grid is painted, tiled or trimmed - every frame is before the walls close.

CRITICAL - SIX PLAIN PHOTOGRAPHS
All six are straight photographs. Nothing superimposed: no panels, cards,
boxes, labels, callouts, annotations, user interface, diagrams, dots, markers,
arrows or lines over any picture.

COMPOSITION
Each cell is 16:9 landscape. Eye level. Verticals true, no converging
distortion, no fisheye, no wide-angle stretch at the edges.

LIGHT AND COLOUR
Flat neutral daylight in every frame, consistent across all six. Grey, dusty,
unglamorous palette. Neutral filmic grade, no saturation push, no HDR, no

AVOID
Lettering, numbers or logos of any kind. Distorted geometry. Any visible seam,
frame or gap between cells. Anything that looks expensive.
