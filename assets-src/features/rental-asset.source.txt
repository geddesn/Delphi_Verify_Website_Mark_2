The stage asset for the Property Rentals scenario of the trust engine. It
replaced a placeholder — the intro townhouse photograph, which was opaque and
rectangular where the stage wants an alpha cut-out standing on bare ground.

TRIMMED TO ITS ALPHA BOUNDING BOX
The supplied canvas carried a transparent margin: content occupied 16,8 to
1427,1077. Extracted to exactly that box on the way in, because the scene's
assetBox and every anchor in it are fractions of the ASSET, and a margin of
unknown width silently makes them fractions of something else. Re-trim on any
replacement, or re-derive the box.

  aspect 1.320:1   (the yacht cut-out is 1.640:1)

WHY 35.5% ON THE STAGE
Every anchor, leader and dot on the trust stage is a percentage of the stage,
so it only points at the building while the building occupies a known box.
35.5% wide at top 61% puts this cut-out's vertical extent at y 37.1-84.9,
which is the yacht's to the tenth — so anchors stay comparable between
sectors — and its horizontal extent at x 32.3-67.8. See assetBox in
src/content/trust-scenes.ts.

All eleven anchors in the scene were verified against this file's own alpha
channel: each one lands on a pixel with alpha 253+, none in the transparent
margin or on the pavement apron.

THE SAME HOUSE AS THE SURVEY
Deliberately consistent with rental-front-elevation (cell 1 of rental-grid):
white stucco, black shutters, columned portico, glossy black door, railings.
The survey opens on that photograph while the stage shows this, and a viewer
who cannot tell they are one building is being shown two properties.

THE GROUND
It brings its own small apron of pavement and kerb, so it stands on something
rather than floating. The perspective floor underneath is still trust-ground,
which was ray-cast from the YACHT's waterline — see the note on ground in
trust-scenes.ts. The two are not derived from the same vanishing point. It
reads acceptably because the apron is small and opaque and hides the join, but
a ground derived from this building's own kerb line would be better.
