WHY A PAIR IN ONE GENERATION RATHER THAN TWO
The Trust Engine argument is that the SAME seat was undamaged at delivery and
damaged at redelivery. Two independent generations would have produced two
different saloons — different leather, different joinery, different window
line — and a viewer reading them as two boats destroys the point. Asking one
generation for both frames is what holds the room, the settee and the cushions
identical between them.

The viewpoints are deliberately similar-but-not-identical: these should read as
two handheld captures weeks apart, not as one photograph retouched.

See the two warnings at the top of src/content/trust-scenes.ts — the damage
must be identical across acts, and Delphi does not decide liability. Neither
frame may imply an adjudication, so no ticks, crosses or before/after labels
were requested, and none appear.
