WHY THIS ONE IS NOT MADE BY THE SCRIPT
Runway cannot return an alpha channel. The parameter exists — gpt_image_2
accepts `background`, unlike every other model on the account — but Runway
exposes only "opaque" | "auto", never OpenAI's "transparent". With "auto" the
model decides, and it decided to PAINT the grey chequerboard that image
editors use to display transparency: a regular 19px grid alternating 235/255,
inside an opaque RGB PNG (colour type 2). It imitated the look of transparency
instead of producing it. Task 89f682b9-b8f4-4783-a1fa-2be317281767.

Asking harder makes it worse. That attempt said "transparent" a dozen times
and then listed "checkerboard" under AVOID, and naming a thing in a prompt
tends to summon it. Both words are gone from the brief below.

The web interface has the real setting, so this one is generated there by hand
and dropped into this folder. Verify before use — hasAlpha must be true:
  node -e "require('sharp')('assets-src/features/yacht-cutout.png').metadata().then(m=>console.log(m.channels, m.hasAlpha))"

A large luxury motor yacht, on its own, with nobody aboard.

THE VESSEL
A well-kept white motor yacht of perhaps forty-five metres, seen from a low
three-quarter angle from forward, roughly at the level of the waterline: the
bow and flare lead the eye and the full run of the topsides and superstructure
falls away toward the stern. The complete vessel is in frame, bow toward the
left of centre, with clear space around every edge — nothing touching or
running off the sides.

Sharp sheerline, raked bow, dark glazing set flush into white composite
superstructure, oiled teak decks, stainless rails and cleats. A tender stowed
and covered on the aft deck. Fenders in. Sunpads bare — no cushions arranged,
no towels, no table laid.

The hull ends cleanly at the waterline, the way a builder's rendering does,
with nothing below it. No sea, no water, no ground, no cradle, no boat stands.

LIGHT AND FORM
Soft, even, neutral light with real shadow structure along the topsides and
under the overhangs, so the vessel reads as a photograph of a solid object
rather than as a flat sticker. Warm neutral palette — bone white, teak,
stainless grey. Restrained and slightly desaturated. Neutral filmic grade, no
saturation push, no orange-and-teal, no HDR flatness.

Shadow on the vessel itself is wanted. There is no surface for it to fall onto,
so there is no shadow beneath the hull.

THE OUTLINE
The silhouette is the important part, so keep every edge crisp and cleanly
resolved: no motion blur, no haze, no bloom, no soft focus, no feathering and
no bright halo anywhere along it. Resolve the thin parts properly — the
stanchions, the handrails, the radar mast and its aerials — rather than
letting them dissolve.

STRICTLY NOT
No people anywhere — not on deck, not on the flybridge, not in a tender, no
figures, no silhouettes, no reflections of people. No guests, no crew, no
champagne, no glassware, no swimwear. No gold fittings, no leopard print. No
sky, cloud, coastline, marina or dock. No sunset, no lens flare. No drone or
high aerial view — the camera is low.

AVOID
Any person, figure or human reflection. Text, lettering, numbers or logos of
any kind anywhere, including vessel names, hailing ports and registration
marks. Any user interface, panel, label, callout, marker, pin, badge or
annotation — these are added afterwards and must not be drawn. Holograms,
floating panels, glowing dots, light trails, network lines, connecting lines.
Blockchain, padlock, shield or fingerprint symbols. Visible third-party brands.
Watermarks. Anything that reads as a scale model, a toy or untextured CGI.
