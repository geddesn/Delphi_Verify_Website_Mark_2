DIRECTION CHANGE — v2 removes the human subject.
v1 showed a captain photographing the deck. This shows the vessel alone at the
moment of redelivery. The industry is carried by the asset and the situation,
not by a person performing an action. Two consequences worth knowing:
  1. Every failure mode in v1 was anatomical - extra arms, warped phone, eye
     contact. Those disappear entirely here.
  2. Scale becomes the new risk. With no figure for reference a large yacht
     reads as a model, so the SCALE block below is load-bearing, not optional.

Photorealistic medium-format photograph: a large motor yacht lying stern-to at
a Mediterranean quay early in the morning, immediately after redelivery, with
nobody aboard.

SCENE
The aft section and transom of a well-kept motor yacht of perhaps forty metres,
berthed stern-to the quay in a working Mediterranean marina near Marbella.
Oiled teak decking laid in neat seams, stainless rails and cleats, white
composite superstructure. The passerelle is lowered to the quay. Mooring lines
are freshly made fast to the bollards and still holding their coil. Fenders are
still out along the hull. A tender cover lies folded on the aft deck. Aft
sunpads are uncovered but empty; no cushions arranged, no table laid.

Beyond the transom: the granite quay edge, cleats and bollards, other masts
further along, the dry Andalusian hills in soft haze.

THE MOMENT
This is the gap between two parties. The charter has ended, the guests have
gone, and the vessel has not yet been checked over and signed for. Everything
should read as very recently active but now still: the deck cleared but not yet
cleaned, lines made fast but not yet tidied, nobody present.

The emptiness is the subject. It should feel like a held breath rather than a
photograph taken before anyone arrived.

STRICTLY NOT
No people anywhere - not on deck, not on the quay, not on neighbouring vessels,
no figures in the background, no silhouettes, no reflections of people. No
champagne, glassware, guests, swimwear, models or party. No gold fittings, no
leopard print, no supercar on the quay, no helicopter. Not a brochure or
charter advertisement. No drone or aerial view. No sunset, no lens flare. Not
Caribbean, not Gulf - this is the Mediterranean coast of Spain.

SCALE - CRITICAL
With no human figure in frame the vessel must still read as genuinely large.
Establish scale through built elements at known size: the width of the teak
seams, the height of the stainless rail, the passerelle treads, quay bollards
and cleats, the diameter of the fenders, the courses of stone in the quay wall.
The transom should feel tall from the quayside viewpoint. Nothing should read
as a scale model, a toy, or a tabletop render.

COMPOSITION - CENTRE-WEIGHTED, READ THIS BEFORE FRAMING
Delivered as 16:9, but the website crops it. On desktop the card renders at
roughly 421×385 - very nearly square - and `object-cover` centre-crops the
source, discarding about 18% of the width from EACH side. Only the central
square survives. On mobile the full 16:9 is shown.

So the frame has two jobs at once:

  SAFE AREA - the central square, i.e. the middle ~64% of the width. The
  vessel, the transom, the lowered passerelle and the mooring lines must all
  sit inside it. This region alone has to read as "large yacht, just handed
  back". Compose it as if it were a square photograph.

  EXTENSION - the outer left and right eighteenths. Quay, further masts, hills,
  open water. Atmosphere and depth only. Nothing here may be load-bearing,
  because desktop visitors will never see it.

Do NOT place the subject in the right third and let the scene open away to the
left. That is the v1 composition, and the centre crop cuts straight through it.

Camera at natural standing eye level on the quay. The transom sits centred and
square-on to slightly off-axis, the passerelle running down toward the viewer
as a leading diagonal that stays within the safe area.

Verticals corrected and true, architectural discipline. One obvious subject,
uncluttered, generous negative space, so the picture reads clearly at small
size on a web page. Transom, passerelle and near deck sharp; the marina beyond
gently soft.

LIGHT AND COLOUR
Warm neutral palette - teak, bone white, stainless grey, muted navy, pale
stone. Soft directional early daylight, low sun, with real shadow structure
across the deck and quay. The sea and sky restrained and slightly desaturated,
never postcard blue. Neutral filmic grade. No saturation push, no
orange-and-teal, no HDR flatness, no artificial fill.

STYLE
Premium commercial photography for a brand campaign - the visual language of a
serious marine title, calm, quiet and understated. High resolution, accurate
naval architecture, correct rigging and deck hardware, believable reflections
in water and stainless.

AVOID
Any person, figure, silhouette or human reflection. Text, lettering, numbers or
